Palm Oil EUDR Traceability & Deforestation-Risk Screening

An end-to-end GIS workflow for EU Deforestation Regulation due diligence in a Nigerian palm-oil supply chain. It links suppliers to plantation polygons, checks the geometry, and screens every plantation and its 10 km surroundings for vegetation loss since the 31 December 2020 cut-off, using land-use/land-cover change. The results are delivered as an ArcGIS Dashboard and a Leaflet.js web map with GeoJSON export.

Overview

Palm-oil buyers exporting to the EU must now prove, plot by plot, that what they buy was not grown on land deforested after 31 December 2020. In Nigeria that is hard. Supply is dominated by smallholders, fruit and oil move through aggregators and millers, and plantation boundaries are rarely recorded.

This project shows how a standard Esri stack can close that gap. Suppliers and plantations are captured in ArcGIS Field Maps, linked in ArcGIS Online and checked in ArcGIS Pro. Each plantation is then screened for vegetation loss between 2020 and 2025 by comparing land-use/land-cover (LULC) classifications. The results are published as an ArcGIS Dashboard for compliance review and an open web map that exports the plantation polygons as GeoJSON.

Problem statement

Under Regulation (EU) 2023/1115, palm oil placed on the EU market must meet two conditions:

  • Deforestation-free: not produced on land converted from forest after 31 December 2020.
  • Geolocated: with polygons for plots larger than 4 ha.

Nigerian supply chains usually meet neither condition. Boundaries are undocumented, a single aggregator or mill sources from many smallholdings, and some plantations have no traceable buyer at all. There is also no systematic way to flag land-cover change since the cut-off. Buyers need a workflow that is cheap, auditable and runnable by their own teams.

Objectives

  1. Design a field-collectable data model that links suppliers (aggregators and millers) to plantation polygons and still supports standalone plantations.
  2. Screen each plantation and its 10 km surroundings for vegetation loss between 2020 and 2025 using LULC change.
  3. Summarise loss area and loss percentage per plantation in an ArcGIS Dashboard built for due-diligence review.
  4. Publish an open web map and export verified plantation geometries and screening attributes as GeoJSON.

Study area

The study area covers oil-palm-producing parts of five southern Nigerian states: Akwa Ibom, Cross River, Ebonyi, Edo and Imo. Screening runs at two scales:

  • Plantation: inside each plantation polygon.
  • Landscape: inside a 10 km buffer around each plantation, which catches clearing in the wider sourcing area.

Interactive map

The map supports these tasks:

  • Filter by state, screening status or supplier.
  • Click a plantation to see its traceability record and its loss inside the polygon and in the 10 km buffer.
  • Rank plantations by loss share.
  • Download the visible plantations as GeoJSON, the format EUDR due-diligence statements use for plot geolocation.

ArcGIS Dashboard

The dashboard presents the same analysis for reviewers. It has these elements:

  • Supplier and plantation-linkage summaries.
  • Area and vegetation-change indicators.
  • A top-five table of plantations by loss share.
  • Filters by state, supplier type, link status, verification, evidence and EUDR screening status.

Methodology

Field Maps→Supplier ↔ plantation link→Geometry QA→ LULC 2020 & 2025→Vegetation-loss raster→Tabulate Area→Dashboard→Leaflet + GeoJSON
  1. Data model: supplier points (type, state, annual volume) and plantation polygons with certification, verification and evidence fields. Plantations link to suppliers through a nullable Supplier_ID, so standalone plantations are allowed.
  2. Geometry verification: Area_ha was recalculated from the polygon geometry in a projected CRS. One test plantation (PLT-005) carried a placeholder area of 35.63 ha but measured about 1,008 ha, roughly 28 times larger. EUDR's 4 ha polygon rule depends on catching exactly this kind of error.
  3. Land-cover classification: the study area was classified into land-use/land-cover classes for 2020, the year of the EUDR cut-off, and for 2025.
  4. Vegetation loss: any pixel in a vegetation class in 2020 that is no longer in a vegetation class in 2025 is counted as loss. Everything else that was vegetated in 2020 is counted as retained vegetation.
  5. Zonal summary: Tabulate Area in a projected CRS gives loss and retained-vegetation hectares per plantation and per 10 km buffer. The loss share is calculated as Loss_Percent = Loss_ha / (Loss_ha + NonLoss_ha) × 100.
  6. Publishing: the results were joined to the plantation and buffer layers and published to ArcGIS Online. They were then built into an ArcGIS Dashboard and this Leaflet map, which reads the service directly and exports GeoJSON in WGS 84.

Deliberate wording. Flags read "potential risk: requires further review", never "deforested". A change out of a vegetation class can also come from oil-palm replanting, harvest, burning, seasonal flooding or classification error. Confirming deforestation needs higher-resolution imagery, history and field checks.

The six plantations with the highest loss share inside the polygon. All 17 are in the map and dashboard.

  • Loss inside plantations is small overall: 236 ha of the 51,482 ha mapped (0.46%). No plantation loses more than 2.2% of its area.
  • One plantation dominates: PLT-069, a standalone Ebonyi plantation, accounts for 166 ha, 70% of all loss inside plantations. It has no supplier link, so it is the clearest case to trace and verify before purchase.
  • The landscape tells a different story: loss in the 10 km buffers is higher than inside the plantations in most cases. It peaks at 3.8% around PLT-046 (Imo), 3.7% around PLT-021 and 3.2% around PLT-020 (both Edo). This is the clearing a plot-only check would miss.
  • Screening status reflects evidence, not loss alone: the two "potential risk" plantations (PLT-014 and PLT-068) are the ones with Review Required evidence status. PLT-069 has the highest loss but clean evidence and NASPON verification. The dashboard lets a reviewer see both signals side by side.

No classification accuracy figures are reported here, because the screening has not been validated against field data.

Limitations

  • Synthetic supply-chain data: the supplier and plantation records are not a real company's. The workflow is the deliverable; the specific flags are not.
  • Vegetation loss is not EUDR deforestation: EUDR uses FAO forest criteria (more than 0.5 ha, trees over 5 m, canopy cover above 10%) and excludes agricultural plantations. The "any vegetation class lost" rule is broader than forest loss, so it over-flags. That suits a first-pass screen but not a final finding.
  • Two-date comparison: comparing two dates can miss clearing followed by regrowth, and errors in either classification carry straight into the loss figures.
  • No accuracy assessment published: there are no omission or commission estimates for the classification or the loss map yet.
  • Overlapping buffers: neighbouring 10 km buffers overlap, so buffer losses cannot be summed across plantations.
  • Next step: cross-check flagged areas against the EU Observatory's JRC Global Forest Cover 2020 map and Hansen/GFW tree-cover loss.
